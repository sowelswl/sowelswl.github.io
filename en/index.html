02 — Path and pivot

Systems train judgment.
Markets correct it.

Moving from quantitative systems to discretionary trading did not mean abandoning models. It meant redefining the work of models, judgment, and AI.

  1. 2023 — Nov 2025

    Quantitative phase · completed

    Built an end-to-end A-share quant workflow

    Developed a CSI 500 index-enhancement system spanning data, factors, portfolio construction, execution, and ongoing live evaluation.

  2. Nov 2025

    Turning point

    Pivot to discretionary trading

    Live experience showed that individual quantitative investing struggled to outperform discretionary trading sustainably. At the same time, I saw the potential of LLM-assisted discretionary decisions and chose to pivot.

  3. Dec 2025 — 2026

    Current phase · in progress

    Discretionary trading + LLM assistance

    I am deepening my A-share discretionary practice and building LLM decision-support and research-automation workflows. Quant systems continue to evolve without taking all of my attention.

  4. 2027 — 2028+

    Planned phase · not a commitment

    Expand to other markets when ready

    After the A-share discretionary and AI-assisted framework is stable, I may explore commodities, convertible bonds, and overseas equities, while gradually adding macro research as decision context. The current priority is depth, not dispersion.

  5. Long term

    Professional vision

    Treat trading as a lifelong craft

    Keep systematic thinking inside a discretionary framework, use AI to expand cognition, and remain personally accountable for positions, risk, and outcomes.

06 — Investment principles

Methods change.
Long-term principles should not.

Quantitative models, discretionary judgment, and AI are tools. The durable constraints behind them are circle of competence, cost, risk, and discipline.

Warren Buffett · Berkshire 2013 letter

Stay simple outside your circle of competence.

Buffett asks professionals to respect their circle of competence and points most non-professionals toward low-cost, broadly diversified, long-term investing. His historical instructions for a family trust used 10% short-term US government bonds and 90% in a low-cost S&P 500 index fund. The transferable lesson is simplicity, cost, and discipline—not mechanically copying an allocation.

Read the original letter ↗

John Bogle · Vanguard

Focus on variables you can control.

Short-term market direction is uncontrollable. Diversification, cost, risk tolerance, and whether you stay with a sound plan are controllable. Long-term investing begins as a discipline of behaviour.

Read Vanguard's source ↗

Howard Marks · Oaktree

Risk control is not risk avoidance.

Investing necessarily involves uncertainty. The work is to identify and analyse risk, control losses you cannot afford, and ask whether the risk you accept is adequately compensated.

Read the original memo ↗

These references describe principles that inform my research and practice. They are not personalized investment advice. Any allocation decision should reflect the investor's objectives, horizon, cash flows, and capacity for risk.
